Solid lines are what happened; dashed are forecasts. The shaded band to the right is the future — CAISO's 7-day-ahead load forecast from OASIS against Open-Meteo's temperature forecast. Drag the slider beneath the chart to scrub, or box-select on the plot to zoom.

Every hour in the window as one point. The curve is V-shaped: demand climbs both above and below the comfort band, which is why load models are built on cooling/heating degree hours rather than on raw temperature. Colour is hour of day — the upper arm is late-afternoon air conditioning.

Top: CAISO's day-ahead load forecast error over time, and its average absolute error by hour of day. Bottom left: Open-Meteo's temperature error by how far ahead it was predicting. Bottom right is the question this project exists to ask — on days when the weather forecast missed, did the load forecast miss too? Each point is one day.

Generation by source, stacked. The overlaid line is solar irradiance at the selected location — it should track the solar band closely, and where it does not, clouds or curtailment are the usual explanation. Negative batteries means charging.
